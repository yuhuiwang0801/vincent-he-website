01 / Context

Why this question?

The challenge is to move from a measured signal to a defensible interpretation. This project examines how the choices made while processing and comparing spectra affect that interpretation.

02 / Contribution

What I did.

I processed one unknown sulfur K-edge XANES spectrum and four reference spectra, compared the candidate compounds, and wrote a nine-page paper on the analysis and its limitations.

Read the methods
  • Subtracted the background, normalized the spectra, and aligned their energy grids.
  • Compared dibenzothiophene (DBT), dibenzyl sulfide, sulfoxide, and sulfone references.
  • Used Excel Solver for constrained linear-combination fitting and evaluated the reliability of the match.

03 / Interpretation

What the work tells us.

The unknown spectrum was most consistent with dibenzothiophene (DBT) within the tested reference set.

What I cannot claim.

A spectral match does not prove molecular identity. The result is bounded by the four-compound reference set, and depends on preprocessing, experimental noise, reference selection, and model assumptions.
